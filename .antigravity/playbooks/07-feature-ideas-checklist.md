# 07-feature-ideas-checklist.md

## Objective
Transform basic user requests into robust, competitive software products. Do not just build the obvious version; suggest high-impact features, improvements, and missing pieces that make the product more useful, polished, competitive, or scalable[cite: 32].

## Feature Ideation & Expansion Categories

### 1. User Accounts and Identity
*   Sign up/login, social login, user profiles, role-based permissions, team accounts, admin access[cite: 32].

### 2. Product Functionality
*   Dashboards, search, filters, sorting, saved items, favorites, history, drafts, autosave, import/export, sharing, collaboration, notifications, file uploads[cite: 32].

### 3. Business & Monetization
*   Pricing tiers, subscriptions, payments, coupons, invoicing, referral systems, onboarding flows, analytics, retention tools[cite: 32].

### 4. Quality & Trust
*   Onboarding walkthroughs, empty states, error recovery, audit logs, activity history, settings page, help center, contact/support options, permissions management[cite: 32].

### 5. AI and Automation
*   AI suggestions, summarization, smart search, recommendations, workflow automation, content generation, classification, chat assistant, predictive helpers[cite: 32].

### 6. Growth
*   SEO pages, landing pages, referral loops, user feedback collection, email capture, waitlist, invite system, social sharing, usage analytics[cite: 32].

### 7. Admin and Operations
*   Admin dashboard, moderation tools, user management, content management, billing management, support tools, metrics dashboard[cite: 32].

## Ideation Behavior & Output Preference
*   Identify the core value of the product, identify missing requirements, suggest better features, suggest differentiators, suggest monetization/growth ideas, suggest operational/admin features, and help convert the idea into a practical roadmap[cite: 32].
*   Prioritize features by impact, separate must-have vs nice-to-have, explain why each feature matters, mention tradeoffs, and tailor ideas to the product type/audience/stage avoiding generic fluff[cite: 32].
*   **For MVPs:** Identify the smallest useful version, recommend the fewest features needed for value, avoid bloated scope, but point out what should come in version 2[cite: 32].
*   **Formatting:** Organize ideas into: must-have, should-have, nice-to-have, future ideas. Include why each feature matters, what should be in the MVP, and what can wait[cite: 32].
